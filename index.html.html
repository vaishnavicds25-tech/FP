01 / The project

Information made easier to reach.

The project explores how QR codes and a hybrid digital library could help village residents discover useful information, access learning material and build a stronger reading culture.

⌕

Why this matters

Useful learning resources may be difficult to discover or access consistently. A simple information point can guide residents towards a shared catalogue, study resources and reading opportunities.

◎

Project approach

Combine community fieldwork with a proposed digital-and-physical library model. QR codes can provide a quick entry point to organised information, while printed books and local support keep the service inclusive.

02 / Proposed solution

A small library, with a bigger reach.

These are proposed features for the hybrid digital library concept, not claims that a live library service is already running.

▤

Digital book shelf

Organised e-books and reading material that can be accessed on a phone or shared device.

⌕

Book catalogue

A searchable list of available books, topics and reading categories for easier discovery.

✎

Exam resources

Study notes, practice material and useful learning links for students preparing for exams.

◷

Reading tracker

A simple way for readers to record books, pages or minutes read and celebrate progress.

Access-first design: use readable language, clear icons, low-data pages and printed alternatives. A QR code should supplement—not replace—help for residents who do not have a smartphone or reliable internet.
03 / Fieldwork snapshot

Listen first. Design better.

The supplied project handout records a five-day study covering 50 households. This fieldwork provides a starting point for understanding community needs around information and reading.

50
households included in the survey
Estimated initial project budget₹53,000
1

Understand the community

Use household conversations and survey responses to learn about reading interests, device access and information needs.

2

Organise the resources

Group books, e-books and exam material into clear categories that residents can browse.

3

Introduce QR access

Place clearly labelled QR codes at accessible community points and explain how to scan them.

4

Review and improve

Collect feedback, check which resources are used and improve the catalogue and reading activities over time.

Research note: the handout provides the sample size, study duration and estimated budget. It does not provide detailed survey percentages or findings, so none are invented here.
